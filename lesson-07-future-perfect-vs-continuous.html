Future Perfect, Future Perfect Continuous & Future Continuous

Finished by then, building up to then, or happening right then — three ways to stand in the future and look around.

1 Warm-up — three more future forms

Lesson 6 covered three ways to talk about the future. Today adds three more — for a specific future point where an action is already finished, still building up, or happening right at that moment.

1. Today's forms are for talking about a specific future point where an action is…

2. "By 6pm, I will have finished the report" tells us the report will be…

3. "By 6pm, I will have been working on the report for six hours" tells us…

4. "At 6pm, I will be finishing the report" tells us…

💡 Same trick as always: find the future time point in the sentence, then ask whether the action is finished by then, still building up to it, or happening right at that moment.

3 The concept

Finished, building up, or happening right then?

Pick a point in the future. Future Perfect looks back from that point at something already finished. Future Perfect Continuous looks back at an activity that's been ongoing, leading up to that point. Future Continuous stands right at that point, picturing an action still in progress. Browse each below, or test yourself with Quick check.

✅ Future Perfect: subject + will have + past participle — e.g. "By the time you read this, I will have left."
✅ Future Perfect Continuous: subject + will have been + verb-ing — e.g. "By 6pm, I will have been working for ten hours straight."
✅ Future Continuous: subject + will be + verb-ing — e.g. "This time next week, I'll be relaxing on a beach."

🎯 The tricky part — by vs. at: "by" and "at" point at completely different pictures of the same future moment.

✅ "By 6pm, I will have finished the report." — done, over, complete before that time. Perfect.
✅ "At 6pm, I will be finishing the report." — still in the middle of it at that exact moment. Continuous.

"By" asks "will it be done in time?" — "at" asks "what will be happening right then?"
🎯 The tricky part — finished result, or ongoing duration? Once you know you need "by [time]," the same choice from Lessons 4 and 5 comes back — are you counting a finished result, or picturing how long the activity's been going?

✅ "By 6pm, she will have written three reports." — a completed, countable result. Future Perfect.
✅ "By 6pm, she will have been writing for six hours." — the ongoing duration itself, no count. Future Perfect Continuous.

Just like present and past perfect continuous, future perfect continuous is also the natural choice when the activity explains a future state: "She'll be exhausted, because she'll have been driving for ten hours straight." Watch for this exact contrast in the practice section below.

How is this different from Lesson 6?

Lesson 6's forms — will, going to, present continuous — answer one question: will this happen? They're about deciding, predicting, or scheduling something. Today's forms do something different: they don't ask whether something happens, they jump forward to a specific future moment and describe what things will look like from there, in relation to another point in time.
