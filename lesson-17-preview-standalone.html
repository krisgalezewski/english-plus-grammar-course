Wish / If Only

The same hypothetical grammar you already know — pointed inward, at regret.

1 Warm-up — no new grammar, new job

"Wish" and "if only" don't introduce any new verb forms — they reuse second and third conditional's if-clauses exactly, just without a result clause attached, to express regret or a strong desire for things to be different.

1. "I wish" and "if only" are used to express…

2. "I wish I were taller" expresses regret about…

3. "I wish I had studied harder" expresses regret about…

💡 Ask yourself: am I imagining a different NOW, or a different THEN? That answers which form you need before you even think about the verb.

3 The concept

Wishing about now

"Wish + past simple" expresses regret about the present — exactly the same hypothetical form used in second conditional's if-clause, just with no result clause attached.

Wishing about the past

"Wish + past perfect" expresses regret about the past — exactly the same hypothetical form used in third conditional's if-clause.

Wishing someone (or something) would change

"Wish + would" is different from the other two — it doesn't match a conditional if-clause at all. It expresses frustration that someone else's behaviour, or an ongoing situation, isn't changing. You almost never use it about yourself.

"If only" — the more emphatic alternative

Same three patterns, same meaning — just stronger, more emotional. Hover over either example.

💡 Contractions apply here too: "I wish I'd studied harder" (had studied), "I wish it'd stop raining" (less common, usually written out as "would"), "if only she'd known sooner."

🎯 The tricky part: "would have" doesn't belong after "wish," even though it feels like it should match third conditional's result clause.

❌ "I wish I would have studied harder." — wrong: "wish" only ever takes past perfect here, never "would have."
✅ "I wish I had studied harder." — past perfect, exactly like third conditional's if-clause.

And "wish + would" is for OTHER people's annoying behaviour, essentially never your own: "I wish I would stop biting my nails" sounds odd — "I wish I didn't bite my nails" (past simple) is the natural version about yourself.
