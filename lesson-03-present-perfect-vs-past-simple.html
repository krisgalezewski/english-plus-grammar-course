Present Perfect vs. Past Simple

A finished chapter — or something that still connects to right now.

1 Warm-up — bringing L1 and L2 together

Lesson 1 was about now. Lesson 2 was about the finished past. Today's tense sits right between them — the past, but still connected to now.

3 The concept

Closed chapter, or still open?

Past Simple is a closed chapter — it happened at a specific, finished time. Present Perfect is an open one — it started in the past, but it's still relevant, still true, or still going right now. Browse each below, or test yourself with Quick check.

✅ Past Simple: subject + verb+ed (or irregular) — e.g. "He joined the company in 2015."
✅ Present Perfect: subject + have/has + past participle — e.g. "She has worked here since 2019."

Present Perfect

🎯 The tricky part: a duration phrase like "for five years" doesn't decide the tense by itself either — it depends on whether that period is still going.

✅ "I have lived in Warsaw for five years." — still living there now. Present perfect.
✅ "I lived in Warsaw for five years." — no longer there; a closed chapter. Past simple.

Same phrase ("for five years") — different tense, because what's true right now is different. Pay attention to this exact contrast in the practice section below.
