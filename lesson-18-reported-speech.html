Reported Speech

Statements, questions, and commands — reporting what someone said, one step removed.

1 Warm-up — one step removed

Reported speech tells someone what was said, without quoting the exact words. When the reporting verb is in the past ("said," "asked," "told"), the reported verb usually shifts one step back in time too.

3 The concept

Reporting a statement

Indirect reports of statements consist of a reporting clause ("she said") and a that-clause (the content itself). We often omit "that," especially in speech and informal writing — "She said she was tired" is just as correct as "She said that she was tired." Formal or written English tends to keep "that" more often, but it's genuinely optional either way. The reported verb also shifts one step into the past, matching a past reporting verb like "said."

Backshift, modals, pronouns, and place words

The full picture of what changes when something is reported — verbs, modals, pronouns, and time or place words all shift together.

Tense backshift, at a glance

"Backshift" just means moving the reported verb one step further into the past than it was in the direct speech — present becomes past, past becomes past perfect, and so on — to show that the words were spoken at an earlier point than now. Hover over any form for a direct-to-reported example.

Modal verbs: some shift, some don't

A few modals backshift just like ordinary verbs. Others are already "fixed" forms and stay exactly the same. Hover over any modal.

Modal perfect forms — deduction about the past

"Must have," "should have," "might have," and the rest already point at the past through "have + past participle," so reporting them needs no further backshift at all — they stay exactly as they were, no matter what tense the reporting verb is in. Hover over any form.

💡 One more, rarer modal: "shall" (mostly formal or British, for first-person suggestions or future) traditionally backshifts to "should" — "I shall return," she said → She said she should return. In practice, most speakers today just report it as "would" instead.

Pronouns shift too

Reported speech is told from a different person's point of view, so pronouns usually change along with the verb. Hover over any pair.
