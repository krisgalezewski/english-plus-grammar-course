3 The concept

Two very different jobs (same split as before)

Past Simple answers "What happened?" — a completed event, done and finished. Past Continuous answers "What was already going on?" — the background scene, or an action already in progress when something else happened. Browse each below, or test yourself with Quick check.

✅ Past Simple: subject + verb+ed (or irregular) — e.g. "The researcher published the paper in 1955."
✅ Past Continuous: subject + was/were + verb-ing — e.g. "She was reviewing the data when she noticed it."

Past Continuous

🎯 The tricky part: a "when" clause doesn't automatically decide the tense either — it depends on what actually happened. Ask yourself: was something already in progress when the interruption hit, or did one thing simply happen, then another?

✅ "When the phone rang, I was taking a shower." — the shower was already in progress; the ring interrupted it. Continuous.
✅ "When the phone rang, I answered it." — one thing, then the next, in sequence. Simple.

Same word ("when"), same structure — different tense, because what's actually happening in each sentence is different. Pay attention to this exact contrast in the practice section below.
💡 Quick reminder from Lesson 1: stative verbs (know, believe, want, seem…) still don't take "-ing," even in the past. ✅ "I knew the answer" — ❌ "I was knowing the answer."
