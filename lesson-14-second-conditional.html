3 The concept

A third kind of "if"

Second conditional steps into a world that isn't real — and probably won't be. Not a general truth, not a realistic possibility, but something hypothetical: imagined, unlikely, or flatly untrue right now. The two clauses can still swap places — try the switch button below.

Alternative modals in the result clause

"Would" is the default, but "could" and "might" fit naturally too, depending on what you mean. Hover over any example.

The decision, in order:

Is it always true? → zero. Is it a real, likely future possibility? → first. Is it hypothetical, unlikely, or imaginary? → second.

An interesting fact — some expressions always use "were"

"If I were you" is a fixed expression — it always takes "were," even in the most casual spoken English, unlike ordinary second-conditional sentences where informal "was" is common ("If she was in charge..."). "As it were" (meaning "so to speak") is another leftover of this same older grammar, frozen into a fixed phrase.

🎯 The tricky part: the past-simple-looking if-clause has nothing to do with the past — it's purely a hypothetical marker.

❌ "If I would have more time, I'd learn Italian." — wrong: "would" doesn't belong in the if-clause.
✅ "If I had more time, I'd learn Italian." — past simple in the if-clause, always, even though the meaning is entirely about now or the future.

And once you're in second-conditional "world," stay there — don't let "will" sneak into the result clause. It's always "would" (or "could"/"might"), matching the hypothetical frame the if-clause already set up.
