3 The concept

The same pattern, every tense

In an active sentence, the subject does the action. In a passive sentence, the subject receives the action — the doer becomes optional, tucked into a "by" phrase or dropped completely. The tense doesn't change at all; only "be" carries it now, instead of the main verb — simple, continuous, or perfect, the pattern always works the same way.

Scroll through every tense, aspect, and modal below — the "be" wheel moves in sync, and everything else in the sentence stays exactly where it is.

View

Use passive when:

  • The doer is unknown — "My phone was stolen last night." (we don't know who)
  • The doer is obvious or unimportant — "Tea is grown in many countries." (doesn't matter exactly who)
  • The focus is the action or result, not who did it — common in formal, scientific, and news writing: "Three arrests have been made."
  • You want to avoid naming or blaming someone — "Mistakes were made" instead of "I made mistakes."
✅ Get-passive (informal): get + past participle — "My bike got stolen yesterday." More casual than "be," especially for sudden or unlucky events: "She got promoted." Negatives and questions borrow "do": "The car doesn't get washed very often." "How did the window get broken?"
🎯 The tricky part: building a correct passive means getting three things right at once — the right tense of "be," the past participle (not the base form), and nothing else changing.

❌ "The cake was make by my grandmother." — wrong: needs the past participle, "made."
❌ "The report has wrote." — wrong: needs "has been written," not just a different past form.
❌ "The rules must followed." — wrong: missing "be" — modals always need "be" before the participle.
❌ "The roof must have damaged by the storm." — wrong: needs "have been damaged" — modal perfect passive needs "been" too, not just "have."

One more sign passive is required, not optional: if the subject genuinely can't perform the action — results can't announce themselves, forms can't submit themselves — passive is the only option that makes sense.

Not every sentence can go passive

Three real limits worth knowing before you try to passivise everything in sight:

1️⃣ Intransitive verbs have no object to promote. A verb like "arrive," "happen," "sleep," or "die" isn't followed by an object — there's nothing to turn into a passive subject.

✅ "The parcel arrived this morning." — active only.
❌ "The parcel was arrived this morning." — there was never an object here to promote.
2️⃣ Perfect continuous passive is technically possible, but avoided. "Have/has/had been being + past participle" is so clumsy that native speakers reword instead.

😬 "The bridge has been being repaired for months." — grammatically built correctly, but nobody actually says this.
✅ "The bridge has been under repair for months." / "They've been repairing the bridge for months." — reworded instead.
3️⃣ Verbs describing a state rarely go passive — "have" (possession), "resemble," "lack," "suit," "cost."

✅ "They have three cars." — active only.
❌ "Three cars are had by them." — technically follows the pattern, but nobody says it this way.
